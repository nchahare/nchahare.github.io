<img src="/assets/img/meinlab2024.JPG"  height="10px" title="Nimesh Chahare">



I'm a scientist, engineer, amateur artist, and much more. Check my [art](/art)/[CV](/cv).

I trained as a mechanical engineer, in material science. Living tissue turned out to be the material I found most interesting. Cells generate their own forces, feel the forces around them, and change what they do in response. I want to know how much of biology is decided that way.

My PhD at [IBEC Barcelona](https://ibecbarcelona.eu/integrative) was on the [mechanics of epithelial tissues](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.06.30.662426.abstract) and [novel ways to engineer them](https://upcommons.upc.edu/handle/2117/401551).

Then I moved to NYC, to [Columbia](https://nerurkar.bme.columbia.edu/), and spent two years looking at the same epithelial mechanics inside developing chick embryos, where the early brain [expands as an active shell](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.bpr.2026.100280).

Now I'm in the [Stuart group](https://www.embl.org/groups/stuart/) at EMBL Barcelona, asking how mechanics guides stem cells as they self-organize into a neural tube — the precursor of the brain.

On side, I'm the convenor of the [International Epithelial Mechanics Fan Club](https://epithelialmechanics.github.io/). It's a small community I started for those who share my interest. [Join us!](https://epithelialmechanics.github.io/contact)

Welcome to my corner of the internet!<br> I like learning new things, hanging out with people, reading books, cooking curries, making origami, and walking around the city.<br>
